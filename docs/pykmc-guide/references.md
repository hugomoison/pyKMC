# Software and potential references

Use the references associated with the exact software and potential used in your work. The links below identify this tutorial's sources; they do not substitute for recording versions or validating a potential for a new application.

- **pyKMC:** [hugomoison/pyKMC source repository](https://github.com/hugomoison/pyKMC) and its linked documentation. Record the branch and commit; use the maintainers' current recommended citation rather than inventing a pyKMC paper.
- **LAMMPS:** [official citation guidance](https://www.lammps.org/cite.html), including A. P. Thompson et al., *LAMMPS — a flexible simulation tool for particle-based materials modeling at the atomic, meso, and continuum scales*, Computer Physics Communications 271, 108171 (2022), [doi:10.1016/j.cpc.2021.108171](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2021.108171).
- **pARTn / ARTn:** M. Poberznik et al., *pARTn: A plugin implementation of the Activation Relaxation Technique nouveau that takes over the FIRE minimisation algorithm*, Computer Physics Communications 295, 108961 (2024), [doi:10.1016/j.cpc.2023.108961](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2023.108961). See the [official documentation and method references](https://mammasmias.gitlab.io/artn-plugin/#references).
- **IRA:** M. Gunde et al., *IRA: A shape matching approach for recognition and comparison of generic atomic patterns*, Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling (2021), [doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00567](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00567). See the [repository's references](https://github.com/mammasmias/IterativeRotationsAssignments#references) for additional methods used by your version.
- **Ni potential:** the header of `Ni_v6_2.0_LKBeland2016.eam` cites R. E. Stoller et al., *Impact of Short-Range Forces on Defect Production from High-Energy Collisions*, Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation 12, 2871–2879 (2016), [doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.5b01194](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5b01194), and the underlying Mishin et al. work, [doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.59.3393](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.59.3393). See the [NIST entry for the restiffened Ni potential](https://www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/entry/2016--Stoller-R-E-Tamm-A-Beland-L-K-et-al--Ni/). Keep using the file copied from the installed example; a related Ni–Co potential is not an interchangeable substitute.
- **OVITO:** A. Stukowski, *Visualization and analysis of atomistic simulation data with OVITO — the Open Visualization Tool*, Modelling and Simulation in Materials Science and Engineering 18, 015012 (2010), [doi:10.1088/0965-0393/18/1/015012](https://doi.org/10.1088/0965-0393/18/1/015012). See [OVITO](https://www.ovito.org/) and the [Wigner–Seitz analysis manual](https://www.ovito.org/manual/reference/pipelines/modifiers/wigner_seitz_analysis.html).
- **Si potential:** F. H. Stillinger and T. A. Weber, *Computer simulation of local order in condensed phases of silicon*, Physical Review B 31, 5262–5271 (1985), [doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.31.5262](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.31.5262). Use the supplied `Si.sw` file; see the [LAMMPS Stillinger–Weber documentation](https://docs.lammps.org/pair_sw.html) for its two- and three-body terms and coefficient syntax.
- **Cu potential:** `Cu01.eam.alloy` is the EAM1 potential of Y. Mishin et al., *Structural stability and lattice defects in copper: Ab initio, tight-binding, and embedded-atom calculations*, Physical Review B 63, 224106 (2001), [doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.63.224106](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.63.224106). Credit the [NIST potential repository](https://www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/entry/2001--Mishin-Y-Mehl-M-J-Papaconstantopoulos-D-A-et-al--Cu-1/) for the LAMMPS conversion by C. A. Becker. Use the file from your installed example, not an arbitrarily substituted Cu potential.

## Fe and NiCr example potentials

- **Fe:** use `FeC-Becquart2013.eam` from the installed example. Its header credits Becquart et al., Computational Materials Science 40 (2007), 119–129, with the Veiga et al. modification. The related published correction is R. G. A. Veiga, C. S. Becquart and M. Perez (2014), *Comments on “Atomistic modeling of an Fe system with a small concentration of C”*, Computational Materials Science 82, 118–121, [doi:10.1016/j.commatsci.2013.09.048](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commatsci.2013.09.048); see the [NIST provenance entry](https://www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/entry/2014--Veiga-R-G-A-Becquart-C-S-Perez-M--Fe-C/). The filename year is not the final publication year. The tutorial contains only Fe atoms; preserve the supplied file rather than replacing it with an arbitrary Fe potential.
- **NiCr:** `Bonny_2013_NiFeCr.eam` cites G. Bonny, N. Castin and D. Terentyev, *Interatomic potential for studying ageing under irradiation in stainless steels: the FeNiCr model alloy*, Modelling and Simulation in Materials Science and Engineering 21, 085004 (2013), [doi:10.1088/0965-0393/21/8/085004](https://doi.org/10.1088/0965-0393/21/8/085004). The [NIST entry](https://www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/entry/2013--Bonny-G-Castin-N-Terentyev-D--Fe-Ni-Cr/) identifies its intended point-defect/alloy scope. Using its Cr/Ni components in this artificial slab demonstrates code behavior; it does not by itself validate NiCr surface energetics.

These entries identify provenance from the supplied headers and literature, not a byte-for-byte certification against a different downloadable tabulation.

## Frenkel-pair recombination

- H. Sun and L. K. Béland, *Statistical distribution of spontaneous recombination radii of Frenkel pairs in FCC and BCC metals*, Acta Materialia 229, 117814 (2022), [doi:10.1016/j.actamat.2022.117814](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2022.117814).
- H. Sun and L. K. Béland, *Calculation of the recombination radii between various point defects and defect clusters in nickel via kinetic Activation Relaxation Technique*, Journal of Nuclear Materials 586, 154663 (2023), [doi:10.1016/j.jnucmat.2023.154663](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnucmat.2023.154663).

These studies provide background on defect encounters and recombination. They do not prescribe a fixed KMC step count, validate this guide's catalog, or turn a literature recombination radius into an `ract`/`rmov` setting. Check the full methodology and potential before comparing numerical predictions.
